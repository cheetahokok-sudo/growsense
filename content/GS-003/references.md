### A. WHO growth systems (authority, no PMID)
1. WHO Child Growth Standards, birth–5 y (length/height-for-age,
   weight-for-age, weight-for-length/height, BMI-for-age). Multicentre
   Growth Reference Study. *(A prescriptive standard — healthy growth.)*
2. WHO Growth reference data for 5–19 years, 2007 (height-for-age,
   weight-for-age to 10 y, BMI-for-age). *(A reference, not the same
   standard — reconstructed from older NCHS data + linked to the under-5
   standards.)*

### B. Why the chart you choose matters
3. El Mouzan MI, et al. Comparison of the 2005 growth charts for Saudi
   children and adolescents to the 2000 CDC growth charts. *Ann Saudi
   Med.* 2008;28(5):334–340. PMID: 18779639. *(CDC chart → higher
   apparent stunting/wasting than the Saudi national chart.)*
4. Inokuchi M, et al. WHO 2006 Child Growth Standards overestimate short
   stature and underestimate overweight in Japanese children. *J Pediatr
   Endocrinol Metab.* 2018;31(1):33–38. PMID: 29267170. *(Classification
   flips with the reference used — the reassurance point.)*
5. Bonthuis M, et al. Use of national and international growth charts for
   studying height in European children. *PLoS One.* 2012;7(8):e42506.
   PMID: 22916131. *(National curves vary; north–south gradient; no single
   "European average".)*
6. Cole TJ, Mori H. Fifty years of child height and weight in Japan and
   South Korea. *Am J Hum Biol.* 2018;30(1):e23054. PMID: 28833849.
   *(Large secular change → charts must be dated and updated.)*
