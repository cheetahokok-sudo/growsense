## Thesis
The four "growth nutrients" all matter because *deficiency* holds a child back —
but correcting a deficiency is not the same as buying height. Beyond adequacy,
none reliably adds centimetres in a well-nourished child. Frames the honest
question as "is my child short of anything?" not "which supplement makes kids
taller?". This is the flagship anti-"height-booster" piece — the moat.

## How the four line up (per-nutrient verdict)
- **Zinc** — the *only* one with a plausible direct linear-growth signal, and
  even that concentrates in deficient/stunted children (Imdad 36994923;
  Monfared 37612628; Roberts&Stein 28298275). Not "the height nutrient" for
  everyone — the nutrient most likely to help a child *short of it*.
- **Calcium** — bone *mineral*, not bone *length*; small, clinically-minor BMD
  gain that fades off-supplement (Winzenberg 16625624); AAP says diet-first
  (Golden 25266429). Low-calcium rickets is the real floor (Munns 26741135).
- **Vitamin D** — essential to prevent rickets, but replete → no height:
  Cochrane little/no effect (Huey 33305842) + two Ganmaa RCTs incl. the 3-yr
  8,851-child trial (28481882, 36441522). Sunlight ≠ reliable dose.
- **Iron** — for oxygen and the brain, not height: no significant growth effect
  (Sachdev 17010257). Respect-most on safety (overdose lethal in toddlers).
