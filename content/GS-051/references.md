# GS-051 · References

> Every load-bearing claim resolves to a real record. Verified against
> PubMed 2026-07-12. 11 PMIDs + 5 authority fact sheets (NIH ODS / WHO,
> cited by name — no PMID). Zero fabrications.

The incoming draft had a 14-item list with **no identifiers**; each was
run down to a real record (or cut). All confirmed.

### A. How nutrients affect growth
1. Roberts JL, Stein AD. The impact of nutritional interventions beyond the
   first 2 years of life on linear growth. *Adv Nutr.* 2017;8(2):323–336.
   PMID: 28298275. *(69 studies; effect depends on baseline status — the
   article's central "adequacy" principle.)*

### B. Calcium & bone
2. Winzenberg T, et al. Calcium supplementation for improving bone mineral
   density in children. *Cochrane* 2006;(2):CD005119. PMID: 16625624.
   *(Only a small upper-limb BMD gain, clinically minor.)*
3. Golden NH, Abrams SA; AAP. Optimizing bone health in children and
   adolescents. *Pediatrics.* 2014;134(4):e1229. PMID: 25266429. *(Diet-first;
   no routine calcium supplementation.)*
4. Munns CF, et al. Global consensus on prevention/management of nutritional
   rickets. *J Clin Endocrinol Metab.* 2016;101(2):394–415. PMID: 26741135.
   *(Low-calcium rickets even without profound vit-D deficiency; infant vit-D.)*

### C. Vitamin D
5. Huey SL, et al. Effects of oral vitamin D on linear growth… under five.
   *Cochrane* 2020;12(12):CD012875. PMID: 33305842. *(Little/no growth effect.)*
6. Ganmaa D, et al. Vitamin D and growth in urban Mongol schoolchildren: two
   RCTs. *PLoS One.* 2017;12(5):e0175237. PMID: 28481882.
7. Ganmaa D, et al. Vitamin D and growth, body composition, pubertal
   development: RCT. *JAMA Pediatr.* 2023;177(1):32–41. PMID: 36441522.
   *(3-yr, 8,851 children, 96% deficient → no growth/puberty effect.)*

### D. Zinc
8. Imdad A, et al. Zinc supplementation for mortality, morbidity, growth
   failure, 6 mo–12 y. *Cochrane* 2023;3(3):CD009384. PMID: 36994923. *(Small
   height gain; benefit where deficiency common.)*
9. Monfared V, et al. Zinc supplementation and anthropometrics in healthy
   children >2 y: SR/MA. *BMC Pediatr.* 2023;23(1):414. PMID: 37612628.
   *(PMID corrected from an inferred value to the real record before publish.)*

### E. Iron
10. Sachdev H, Gera T, Nestel P. Iron supplementation and physical growth in
    children: SR of RCTs. *Public Health Nutr.* 2006;9(7):904–920.
    PMID: 17010257. *(No significant growth effect.)*
11. Baker RD, Greer FR; AAP. Diagnosis/prevention of iron deficiency, 0–3 y.
    *Pediatrics.* 2010;126(5):1040–1050. PMID: 20923825. *(Anchors the
    milk-displaces-iron point; replaces the draft's unverifiable "AAP 2026".)*

### F. Reference intakes & guidance (authority fact sheets)
12–15. NIH Office of Dietary Supplements — Calcium / Vitamin D / Zinc / Iron
    Health Professional Fact Sheets. *(RDAs and tolerable upper limits.)*
16. WHO — Use of multiple micronutrient powders for point-of-use fortification
    in infants and young children. *(Population supplementation programmes.)*
