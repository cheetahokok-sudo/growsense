**Slide 1 — Hook**
Two 8-year-olds. Same age. Very different heights.
Is that... normal?
_(sub: A pediatric growth thread 🧵 · GrowSense)_

**Slide 2 — Reframe**
Doctors don't ask "how tall today?"
They ask: **how fast is the child growing?**
That's *growth velocity* — cm per year.

**Slide 3 — The movie, not the snapshot**
Two healthy kids, both 125 cm at age 8.
One reaches 131, the other 128 by age 9.
Both fine. Growth comes in **waves.**

**Slide 4 — Genetics**
60–80% of height is inherited. _(ref 3)_
Hundreds of genes, not one. _(ref 2)_
Genes set the boundaries — life decides where inside them.

**Slide 5 — Target range**
Boys: (mum + dad + 13) ÷ 2
Girls: (mum + dad − 13) ÷ 2
→ a **range of ±5–7 cm**, not a fixed number.

**Slide 6 — Sleep**
The biggest growth-hormone pulses happen in **deep sleep.** _(ref 1)_
Late nights and broken sleep cut them short.
Protecting deep sleep genuinely matters.

**Slide 7 — Fuel**
Protein builds the bone matrix and drives IGF-1.
Zinc supports growth; deficiency is tied to stunting. _(ref 7)_
Calcium hardens; vitamin D lets the gut absorb it.

**Slide 8 — The honest one**
Exercise does **not** stretch bones taller.
It builds **stronger, denser** bones. _(ref 5, 7)_
(Anyone promising height from a supplement? Be skeptical.)

**Slide 9 — Late bloomers**
Lower percentile but **steady velocity**? Usually healthy.
Some kids just have a later skeletal clock —
and keep growing into their late teens.
