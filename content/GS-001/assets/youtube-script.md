**[0:00 · HOOK]**
Picture a classroom of eight-year-olds. One kid's shot up three sizes since
last year. Another hasn't changed since… forever. Same age. And here's the
thing most parents don't hear enough: **that's usually completely normal.**
`[VISUAL: hero graphic — two same-age kids on a soft growth curve]`

**[0:25 · PROMISE]**
In the next few minutes I'll show you what *actually* drives how fast a child
grows — genes, hormones, sleep, food — and what you can realistically do.
Everything here is sourced; you'll see the references on screen. No fear, no
"miracle height" nonsense.

**[0:45 · VELOCITY]**
Parents ask "how tall is my child?" Doctors ask something smarter: "how *fast*
are they growing?" That's growth velocity — centimeters per year.
`[VISUAL: the two-child table — both 125cm at 8, then 131 vs 128 at 9]`
A single measurement is a snapshot. Velocity is the whole movie. Growth comes
in **waves.**

**[1:30 · GENETICS]**
Sixty to eighty percent of height is inherited — and it's not one gene, it's
hundreds. `[TEXT: 60–80% heritable · ref 3 · Lango Allen et al. Nature 2010 · ref 2]`
There's a simple target-range formula — but notice it gives a *range*, plus or
minus 5 to 7 centimeters, not a guarantee. `[VISUAL: mid-parental formula]`

**[2:30 · GH–IGF-1]**
Genes are the blueprint; hormones are the construction crew. The pituitary
releases growth hormone in pulses, the liver turns that into IGF-1, and IGF-1
tells the growth plates to divide. `[VISUAL: GH → liver → IGF-1 → growth plate flow]`

**[3:15 · SLEEP]**
Here's the one parents can actually influence tonight: the biggest
growth-hormone pulses happen in **deep sleep.** `[TEXT: ~70% of sleep GH pulses in deep (N3) sleep · ref 1]`
Late bedtimes and broken sleep cut those windows short. Protecting deep sleep
isn't a wives' tale — it's physiology.

**[4:00 · NUTRITION]**
Growth needs raw materials: protein for the bone matrix and IGF-1, zinc for
tissue growth `[TEXT: zinc → linear growth · ref 7]`, and calcium plus vitamin
D to harden and absorb.

**[4:40 · THE HONEST BIT]**
Now the myth I need to kill: exercise does **not** stretch bones taller. What
it does — and this is real and valuable — is build **stronger, denser** bones.
`[VISUAL: activity graphic — "stronger bones, not taller ones"]`
So if anyone's selling you height in a bottle… you already know.

**[5:20 · LATE BLOOMERS]**
Lower percentile but steady velocity is usually a healthy kid — sometimes just
a late bloomer whose skeletal clock runs behind. Which is why doctors also
check **bone age** — an X-ray of the hand and wrist that shows how much growth
runway is left. `[TEXT: bone age · ref 8]`

**[6:00 · WHAT TO DO + CTA]**
So, realistically: protect deep sleep, prioritize protein, encourage
weight-bearing play, and track **velocity over months** — not one anxious
number at school. And if velocity drops below about 4 cm a year, or the line
flattens, that's your cue to check with your pediatrician. `[TEXT: ref 6]`
