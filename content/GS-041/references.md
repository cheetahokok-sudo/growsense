## Verified — checked against PubMed, 2026-07-12

**All 19 citations in the author's draft resolved to real records (no

PubMed titles (several draft entries were paraphrased). Each is uniquely
locatable by its PMID.

### A. Exercise & growth — overview

1. **Alves JGB, Alves GV.** Effects of physical activity on children's growth.
   *J Pediatr (Rio J).* 2019. PMID: 30593790.
   *(Physical activity is safe and does not impair linear growth.)*
2. **Borer KT.** The effects of exercise on growth. *Sports Med.* 1995.
   PMID: 8614759.
3. **Rogol AD, Clark PA, Roemmich JN.** Growth and pubertal development in
   children and adolescents: effects of diet and physical activity. *Am J Clin
   Nutr.* 2000;72(2 Suppl):521S–528S. PMID: 10919954.
   *(The energy-budget framing: diet + activity together.)*
4. **Torun B, Viteri FE.** Influence of exercise on linear growth. *Eur J Clin
   Nutr.* 1994. PMID: 8005085.
   *(CONTEXT: undernourished children recovering from protein-energy
   malnutrition — activity aided catch-up growth. Does NOT generalise to
   well-fed children; used only for the malnutrition caveat.)*
5. **Bull FC, Al-Ansari SS, Biddle S, et al.** World Health Organization 2020
   guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour. *Br J Sports Med.*
   2020;54(24):1451–1462. PMID: 33239350.
   *(60 min/day MVPA for ages 5–17 + bone-strengthening ≥3×/week.)*

### B. Bone loading & accrual (the "stronger bones" evidence)

6. **Hind K, Burrows M.** Weight-bearing exercise and bone mineral accrual in
   children and adolescents: a review of controlled trials. *Bone.*
   2007;40(1):14–27. PMID: 16956802.
7. **Specker B, Thiex NW, Sudhagoni RG.** Does exercise influence pediatric
   bone? A systematic review. *Clin Orthop Relat Res.* 2015. PMID: 26208606.
   *(~0.6–1.7% greater annual bone accrual, concentrated pre-puberty.)*
8. **Bailey DA, McKay HA, Mirwald RL, Crocker PR, Faulkner RA.** A six-year
   longitudinal study of the relationship of physical activity to bone mineral
   accrual in growing children (Saskatchewan). *J Bone Miner Res.* 1999.
   PMID: 10491214. *(Active girls ~17% / boys ~9% more bone mineral.)*
9. **Khan K, McKay HA, Haapasalo H, et al.** Does childhood and adolescence
   provide a unique opportunity for exercise to strengthen the skeleton? *J Sci
   Med Sport.* 2000. PMID: 11104307. *(Mechanotransduction; the responsive window.)*
10. **MacKelvie KJ, McKay HA, Khan KM, Crocker PR.** A school-based exercise
    intervention augments bone mineral accrual in early pubertal girls. *J
    Pediatr.* 2001. PMID: 11598595. *(Early puberty = opportune window.)*
11. **MacKelvie KJ, Khan KM, Petit MA, Janssen PA, McKay HA.** A school-based
    exercise intervention elicits substantial bone health benefits: a 2-year
    randomized controlled trial in girls. *Pediatrics.* 2003. PMID: 14654643.
12. **Fuchs RK, Bauer JJ, Snow CM.** Jumping improves hip and lumbar spine bone
    mass in prepubescent children: a randomized controlled trial. *J Bone Miner
    Res.* 2001;16(1):148–156. PMID: 11149479.
    *(100 jumps/session, 3×/week, 7 months → +4.5% femoral neck, +3.1% spine.)*
13. **Scerpella TA, Dowthwaite JN, Rosenbaum PF.** Sustained skeletal benefit
    from childhood mechanical loading. *Osteoporos Int.* 2011. PMID: 20838772.
14. **Weaver CM, Gordon CM, Janz KF, et al.** The National Osteoporosis
    Foundation's position statement on peak bone mass development and lifestyle
    factors. *Osteoporos Int.* 2016;27(4):1281–1386. PMID: 26856587.

### C. Specific activities

15. **Tenforde AS, Fredericson M.** Influence of sports participation on bone
    health in the young athlete: a review of the literature. *PM R.* 2011.
    PMID: 21944303. *(High-impact/odd-impact sports build most bone.)*
16. **Gomez-Bruton A, Montero-Marin J, González-Agüero A, et al.** The effect of
    swimming during childhood and adolescence on bone mineral density: a
    systematic review and meta-analysis. *Sports Med.* 2016. PMID: 26607734.
    *(Swimmers' BMD ≈ sedentary controls, below weight-bearing athletes.)*
17. **Falk B, Eliakim A.** Resistance training, skeletal muscle and growth.
    *Pediatr Endocrinol Rev.* 2003. PMID: 16437017.
    *(Resistance training does not stunt growth; strength via neural adaptation;
    raises IGF-1. NOTE: draft had no year — it is 2003.)*

### D. Intensive training, puberty & fuelling

18. **Malina RM, Baxter-Jones AD, Armstrong N, et al.** Role of intensive
    training in the growth and maturation of artistic gymnasts. *Sports Med.*
    2013;43(9):783–802. PMID: 23743792.
    *(Adult height is NOT compromised by intensive gymnastics — selection +
    natural variation, not stunting. The selection-trap anchor.)*
19. **Roemmich JN, Richmond RJ, Rogol AD.** Consequences of sport training
    during puberty. *J Endocrinol Invest.* 2001. PMID: 11716157.
20. **Bertelloni S, Ruggeri S, Baroncelli GI.** Effects of sports training in
    adolescence on growth, puberty and bone health. *Gynecol Endocrinol.* 2006.
    PMID: 17145646.
21. **Georgopoulos NA, Roupas ND, Theodoropoulou A, et al.** The influence of
    intensive physical training on growth and pubertal development in athletes.
    *Ann N Y Acad Sci.* 2010. PMID: 20840251.
22. **Gould RJ, Ridout AJ, Newton JL.** Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport
    (RED-S) in adolescents — a practical review. *Int J Sports Med.* 2023.
    PMID: 36122585.

## Honesty notes carried into the article
- The **1.0 / 0.65 / 0.35 / 0.15 "loading weights" are GrowSense's own app tiers**,
  framed as a product ranking *informed by* the impact literature (refs 15, 6) —
  **not** a published "osteogenic score."
- **Torun (ref 4)** is a malnutrition-recovery context and is caveated as such —
  never used to imply exercise adds height in well-fed children.
- **Intensive-training refs (18–22)** support the honest line: the risk is
  **under-fuelling (RED-S)**, not exercise itself; final height generally is not
  compromised.
