**[0:00 · HOOK]**
Walk down any supplement aisle and you'll see it: powders and shakes promising to
help your child "grow tall." So let's answer it straight: does more protein make
a child taller? The honest answer — **protein is essential, but it's fuel, not a
height dial.** `[TEXT: protein = fuel, not a height dial · ref 1]`
`[VISUAL: hero — a colorful plate of eggs, milk, tofu, fish, beans]`

**[0:30 · BLUEPRINT VS BRICKS]**
Think of building a house. Your child's **genes are the blueprint** — they set
the final height. Protein is the **bricks.** Run out of bricks, and construction
stalls — that's what protein deficiency does to growth. But here's the key:
dumping *extra* bricks on the site doesn't build the house taller than the plan.
`[VISUAL: blueprint + brick pile]` `[TEXT: excess protein ≠ extra height · ref 5]`

**[1:20 · PROTEIN 101]**
Quick primer. Protein is really chains of **amino acids.** `[VISUAL: chain → snipped into beads]`
Twenty of them; your child's body can make eleven. The other **nine are
essential** — they have to come from food every single day, because the body
can't make them or store them. Those nine are what decide whether a food really
supports growth.

**[2:20 · QUALITY OVER QUANTITY]**
And this is the part most people miss: it's not just *how much* protein, it's the
**quality.** When researchers looked at stunted children, they were low in all
nine essential amino acids — not just low on calories. `[TEXT: stunting ↔ low essential amino acids · ref 15]`
A complete protein — egg, milk, soy — has all nine. Most single plants are low in
one: grains lack lysine, beans lack methionine. The fix? Combine them. Rice plus
beans is complete *together.* `[VISUAL: rice + beans = ✓ complete]`

**[3:30 · THE IGF-1 BRIDGE]**
Why does protein matter for height at all? A hormone called **IGF-1.** Protein
helps drive it, IGF-1 tells the growth plates to divide.
`[VISUAL: pituitary → liver → IGF-1 → growth plate]` `[TEXT: ref 1, 9]`
Animal protein — especially dairy — nudges IGF-1 a bit higher. But above a healthy
intake, more protein doesn't mean more height. The engine's already fuelled.

**[4:30 · HOW MUCH — LESS THAN YOU THINK]**
So how much does a child actually need? Less than most parents assume.
`[TEXT: 4–8 y ~19 g/d · 9–13 y ~34 g/d · ref 11]`
A twenty-kilo child does great on twenty to thirty grams a day — one egg, a cup of
milk, a piece of fish or a bowl of lentils. No powder required.
`[VISUAL: a simple day of food adding up to ~26 g]`

**[5:30 · THE MILK QUESTION]**
"But doesn't milk make kids tall?" A little. The best trials show about
**0.4 centimeters of extra growth a year per glass of milk** — and mostly in kids
who were *short on quality protein to begin with.* `[TEXT: ~0.4 cm/yr per glass · ref 12]`
In a well-fed child, the effect is small, and more milk isn't more height — it's
just more calories.

**[6:20 · MYTHS + BONE]**
Three quick myth-busters. A protein powder won't help a healthy kid grow — and it
skips the zinc, iron and calcium that whole foods bring. "Just feed more" adds
weight, not height. And no — protein doesn't harm bones; it actually *helps* them,
alongside calcium. `[TEXT: protein supports bone · ref 16]`

**[7:00 · THE SYSTEM + CTA]**
Here's the real takeaway: growth is a **whole system.** The best protein in the
world can't fix short sleep or no activity — and the growth-hormone that uses
that protein is released mostly in **deep sleep.** So don't chase grams. Support
