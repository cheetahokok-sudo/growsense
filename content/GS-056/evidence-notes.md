## Thesis
The three foods Asian/Gulf families blame most — fried chicken, milk, soy — are
**not** proven triggers of early puberty. The one consistent *modifiable* factor
is **body fat / energy balance**, working through the leptin gate on the HPG
axis. On-brand: the honest answer is both more reassuring (keep the good foods)
and more useful (watch energy balance; take real early signs to a clinic, not the
kitchen). Marquee Myths-cluster pillar for the primary markets; companion to
[[GS-017]].

## Load-bearing claims → refs
- Higher body fat / energy surplus = most consistent modifiable factor for earlier
  puberty (advanced BA, earlier menarche) → r1, r3, r4.
- Mechanism: leptin from fat = permissive signal on HPG; adiposity → hypothalamic
  inflammation nudges switch earlier → r5, r2.
- **Chicken:** added hormones banned in poultry (US since 1950s; label-law proof);
  broiler speed = breeding + feed, not hormones. Indirect link only via calories →
  r6, r1.
- **Milk:** modestly raises IGF-1 (real) BUT milk intake after age 9 does NOT
  predict menarche age → r8, r7. (Growth-support, not puberty trigger; link [[GS-052]].)
- **Soy:** best human cohort → higher soy = *later* puberty, both sexes (fear runs
  backwards); normal-intake isoflavones weak/mixed → r9.
- **Chemicals:** the real EDC concern is phthalates/bisphenols from packaging, not
  the food's biology; associational, dose-dependent → r10.
- **COVID surge:** real CPP referral jump 2020–22, but attributed to weight gain /
  inactivity / screens / stress — lifestyle, not a food → r11, r12.
- Genuine early signs deserve work-up (timing affects adult height, [[GS-017]]);
  premature thelarche = benign variant to distinguish → r13, r14.

## Honesty choices
- The chicken-hormone myth is refuted with a **regulatory** source (FDA), and the
  *honest caveat* (fried chicken → calories → adiposity) is kept — we don't
  pretend fast food is neutral, we locate the real mechanism.
- Soy section deliberately leads with the counter-intuitive true finding (later,
  not earlier) rather than a defensive "no evidence" — stronger and honest.
- Milk framed as growth-supporting (IGF-1) yet not a puberty trigger — avoids both
  the fear and the overclaim; hands off to [[GS-052]] for the height question.
- Central message repeatedly redirects from "cut a food" to "energy balance +
  see a doctor for real signs" — the anti-fear brand play, and it names the real
  harm of the myth (kitchen instead of clinic).

## The reference story
Built **reference-first**: PubMed esearch→esummary per food *before* prose, so no
fabricated-PMID cleanup was needed (unlike [[GS-019]]/[[GS-017]] drafts). The
soy direction was verified from the abstract (Xiong 35369873) before writing the
claim. "Hormones in chicken" returned **no** supporting PubMed hits — absence is
the finding; anchored to the FDA fact sheet instead.
