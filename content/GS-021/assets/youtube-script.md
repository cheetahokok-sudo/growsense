**[0:00 · HOOK]**
Your kid went to bed way too late, and the guilt kicks in: "Did we just miss the
growth-hormone window?" So let me put you at ease straight away — **almost
certainly not.** `[TEXT: one late night doesn't cost height · ref 6]`
`[VISUAL: hero — a child sleeping peacefully in soft morning light]`

**[0:30 · THE KERNEL OF TRUTH]**
The old "sleep early or you won't grow" advice isn't totally wrong. Growth
hormone comes out in **pulses**, and one of the biggest arrives right after you
fall asleep, in deep sleep. `[VISUAL: GH pulse spiking at sleep onset]`
`[TEXT: major GH pulse follows sleep onset · ref 1, 3]` That's the real link
between sleep and growth.

**[1:20 · THE MIDNIGHT MYTH]**
But here's the myth I need to kill: "growth hormone is only released before
midnight." Not true. Sleep-lab studies show the pulse follows **sleep onset —
not the clock.** `[TEXT: onset, not the clock · ref 1, 3]` Fall asleep later, the
pulse just shifts later. `[VISUAL: two timelines, 10pm vs midnight, same pulse shape]`
Bedtime still matters — but mostly because late nights cut *total* sleep.

**[2:30 · ONE BAD NIGHT]**
"But we lost hours last night!" Relax. When sleep is cut short, the body makes up
the growth hormone **during the day.** `[TEXT: 24-h GH preserved · ref 6]`
Growth happens over months and years — not single nights. Patterns, not
perfection.

**[3:20 · DOES MORE SLEEP = TALLER?]**
So if a late night doesn't hurt… does an *extra* hour help? Here's the honest
answer: there's no reliable "extra hour equals extra centimetres" effect.
`[TEXT: no simple dose-response · ref 10, 11]` Enough, regular sleep supports the
system — it doesn't override genetics.

**[4:20 · THE ONE PLACE IT REALLY MATTERS]**
Now the part every parent should know. There *is* one place sleep truly affects
growth: **obstructive sleep apnea** — often from big tonsils and adenoids.
`[VISUAL: snoring child, airway obstruction]` `[TEXT: OSA can impair growth · ref 12]`
Watch for loud nightly snoring, breathing pauses, gasping, mouth breathing, and
restless, sweaty sleep.

**[5:20 · THE GOOD NEWS]**
And here's the encouraging part: when kids have the obstruction treated — usually
removing the tonsils and adenoids — studies show **catch-up growth** and rising
IGF-1. `[TEXT: catch-up growth + IGF-1 · ref 13, 22]` It doesn't *make* them
taller — it **removes the obstacle** so normal growth resumes.

**[6:20 · YOUR SMARTWATCH]**
One quick myth-buster: your Fitbit, Apple Watch or Oura estimate sleep stages —
they do **not** measure growth hormone. A low "deep sleep score" does not mean
your child failed to grow. Great for trends, useless for diagnosis.

**[7:00 · THE TAKEAWAY + CTA]**

healthy breathing, and if loud nightly snoring ever shows up alongside slowing
