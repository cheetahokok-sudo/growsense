# GS-005 · Instagram Carousel (10 slides)

Format 4:5 (1080×1350). Voice: calm, reassuring, science-backed — the smart
friend who's also a scientist. This is the **reassurance** flagship, so the

**Slide 1 — Hook**
Your child is the shortest in the class.
Should you worry?
_(sub: A calm, science-backed thread 🧵 · GrowSense)_

**Slide 2 — The reassurance up front**
Most short children are **completely healthy.** _(ref 1)_
Usually it's shorter family genes, or a **late bloomer** —
not a medical problem.

**Slide 3 — The percentile paradox**
"3rd percentile" isn't a diagnosis.
By definition, 3 in every 100 healthy kids
sit at the bottom of the chart. Someone has to. _(ref 12, 13)_

**Slide 4 — The mindset shift**
Doctors don't compare your child to classmates.
They compare your child to **their own curve.**
"Shortest in class" is social. "Falling off their curve" is medical.

**Slide 5 — The one number that matters**
Not height today — **growth velocity.**
A short kid growing +5 cm/yr = healthy.
A taller kid growing +2 cm/yr = worth a check. _(ref 6)_

**Slide 6 — Your family's zone**
Boys: (mum + dad + 13) ÷ 2
Girls: (mum + dad − 13) ÷ 2
Most kids finish within **±8.5 cm** of that centre. _(ref 11)_
Track toward *your* family's zone — not the average.

**Slide 7 — The late bloomer**
Normal growth rate + delayed bone age + family history
= a classic late bloomer. _(ref 8)_
Many of the tallest adults were the smallest kids in middle school.

**Slide 8 — The real red flags** _(save this one)_
See a doctor if you notice:
• Crossing percentiles **downward** _(ref 15)_
• Velocity under **~4 cm/yr** _(ref 2)_
• No puberty by 13 (girls) / 14 (boys) _(ref 7)_
• Slow growth **+** feeling unwell

**Slide 9 — The honest one**
No supplement or powder raises a healthy child's final height.
Growth-hormone shots? A real medicine for real diagnoses —
**not** a booster for healthy short kids. _(ref 2, 3)_

**Slide 10 — Soft CTA**
Your child's chart is a **compass, not a report card.**
GrowSense tracks velocity & your child's own trajectory —
honestly labelling measured vs. estimated.
_(Save this · Follow for the science, minus the fear)_
