# GS-052 · Evidence notes

**Article:** Does Milk Make Kids Taller? What Dairy Really Does for Growth
**Cluster:** Myths · **Published slug:** /blog/does-milk-make-kids-taller
**Verified:** 2026-07-12 · 13/13 citations real & on-topic · 0 fabrications

## Thesis
Milk *supports* growth; it does not *create* it. It removes nutritional
barriers so a child can express existing potential — it cannot raise the
genetic ceiling. This keeps the article inside house honesty limits (contrast
with the seductive "drink milk → grow tall" folk claim).

## Central numbers (all anchored to real papers)
- **~0.4 cm/year per ~245 mL/day extra dairy** — de Beer meta-analysis
  (PMID 21890437), independently reproduced at **+0.39 cm per 236 mL/day** in a
  17-year birth cohort (Marshall, PMID 29924327). Two independent designs, same
  magnitude → high confidence in the *effect size*, not just direction.
- **Effect concentrates in the undernourished** — Herber (668k children, LMIC,
  PMID 32317668) shows stunting reduction; Wiley NHANES (PMID 15981182) shows
  ~no height effect in well-nourished 5–11 y. This is the threshold principle.
- **Density ≠ length** — Hidayat 2023 RCT meta (PMID 37414219): dairy adds
  meaningful bone *mineral* but only ~+0.21 cm height. The "density paradox"
  that anchors §8.
- **Plant-based swap ≈ −0.8 cm at 3 cups/day** — Soczynska 2024 (PMID 38219863)
  cohort + systematic review (PMID 39332772). Framed as "replace the matrix."

## Two things I deliberately did NOT overclaim
1. **IGF-1 → taller adult.** The pathway (milk protein → GH activity → IGF-1 →
   growth-plate division) is real and cited (Hoppe 15277169; Grenov 32211798,
   34333391), but higher IGF-1 does not *guarantee* a taller adult. Stated
   explicitly in §4.
2. **Early puberty.** Evidence does not justify avoiding normal milk; the
   stronger driver of earlier puberty is excess adiposity. No citation forced
   where the literature is genuinely mixed — claim kept qualitative (§11).

## Cross-topic consistency check
Newer papers agree with the older meta-analyses in *direction and size*:
de Beer 2012 ↔ Marshall 2018 ↔ Herber 2020 all land small-positive, larger in
deficit. de Lamas 2019 ↔ Hidayat 2023 both split bone-mineral (strong) from
length (weak). No cherry-picking of a lone outlier.
