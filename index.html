Activity & mechanical load
HIGH IMPACT activities (gymnastics, basketball, jumping, martial arts) are the strongest osteogenic stimulus — they directly load growth plates and trigger GH/IGF-1 release. Pre-puberty is the critical window. CARDIO (swimming, cycling) is excellent for heart health but has minimal bone growth benefit — combine with weight-bearing. Aim for 60 min HIGH IMPACT daily.

Medications
parent-reported
Corticosteroids (inhaled or oral) are the main medication class that affects growth — they can suppress the hypothalamic-pituitary axis and reduce growth velocity with prolonged use. This is NOT a reason to avoid necessary medication; it gives context for interpreting slow growth periods. Inhaled steroids for asthma at standard doses have minimal growth impact.

Lab values
measured
IGF-1 is the best single biomarker for growth hormone axis activity — low IGF-1 at normal height velocity suggests the body is growing efficiently; low IGF-1 with slow growth warrants endocrinology referral. Vitamin D affects bone mineralisation and growth plate maturation — deficiency is common in children with limited outdoor time. Ferritin (iron stores) affects energy and is routinely checked in pediatric growth assessments. Reference ranges vary by lab and age — always compare against your lab's printed range.

Puberty milestones
Puberty timing significantly changes how the growth chart should be interpreted. An early growth spurt can make a child look on-track while epiphyseal plates fuse sooner than average. A delayed puberty can look like slow growth but may mean more growing time remains. Tanner staging gives the pediatrician context for all measurements. Log milestones as you observe them — even retrospectively.
Tracking pubertal timing — one of the strongest real predictors of remaining growth window and adult height. Record what your pediatrician observes or what you notice at home.

Bone age assessment
X-ray
A bone age X-ray measures the maturation of wrist growth plates against the Greulich-Pyle (GP) atlas. Bone age significantly behind chronological age ("delayed") often means more height potential remains. Bone age ahead ("advanced") may indicate early puberty or hormonal factors. The app calculates the statistical delta and SD from your radiologist's report — bring this screen to your next endocrinology visit.
